**As of 27 November 2026.** The dates and rules below come from Campus France’s official France Excellence Eiffel pages and from the 2027 application guide and scholarship regulations, all read on that day. Institutions set earlier internal deadlines, and documents can be updated, so confirm anything that affects your file with the French institution you hope will nominate you, and on the linked Campus France page.

This is a deadline and process guide. It is not a Campus France undergraduate calendar; country dates for Morocco, Tunisia and Egypt are in [Campus France 2027: which French test you need in Morocco, Tunisia and Egypt](/blog/campus-france-2027-french-test-maroc-tunisie-egypte). Language tests are only a side note here.

## You cannot send the application yourself

The [programme page](https://www.campusfrance.org/en/france-excellence-eiffel-scholarship-program) is blunt: **only applications submitted by French higher education institutions are accepted.**

The [student-facing Eiffel page](https://www.campusfrance.org/en/the-france-excellence-eiffel-scholarship-program) adds the practical steps, and this line: applications sent directly by students or by foreign institutions will be declared ineligible. The 2027 institutional [application guide](https://www.campusfrance.org/system/files/medias/documents/2026-10/guide_depot_candidatures_eiffel_2027_V2.pdf) repeats that only French higher-education and research institutions may submit files, and that a candidate presented by more than one institution will be rejected.

What you can do is:

1. Choose a French school or university that fits your plans. Campus France says all French higher education institutions can submit applications.
2. Contact that institution’s **international relations** office — through its website, by email or by telephone — and ask how and when it nominates Eiffel candidates. Also contact Campus France in your country, or the Cooperation and Cultural Action Department of the French embassy, for guidance on your study plans.
3. If the institution accepts and supports your application, **it** submits the file online through Campus France.

That is the whole application route. There is no student portal for sending an Eiffel file on your own.

## The 2027 timeline

The official campaign timeline on the programme page is short:

- **Opening of the call for applications:** October 2026
- **Deadline for Campus France to receive applications:** **6 January 2027**, included
- **Publication of results:** from **12 April 2027**

The 2027 application guide gives the same institutional deadline for online deposit on the SMApply platform: **Wednesday 6 January 2027 included**. Files received after that date are not evaluated.

One wording clash is worth flagging. A Campus France [news item](https://www.campusfrance.org/en/actu/call-for-france-excellence-eiffel-applications-2027) opens with “open until January 8, 2027,” then states in the next sentence that the call is open until **6 January 2027** and is exclusively for French institutions. This post follows the official programme timeline and the application guide: **6 January 2027 included**.

That 6 January date is when **the institution** must have submitted the file. Many universities close their own calls days or weeks earlier, so that they can preselect candidates and complete the dossier. Campus France does not publish those internal dates; you have to ask the institution. With the Campus France deadline now a little over five weeks away (as of 27 November 2026), this is the time to write to international relations, not the time to start looking for a host after the new year.

The guide also says each institution may present no more than 40 applications per field of study and per track (master’s or doctorate). That is one reason institutions preselect.

## Who the official pages say is eligible

The programme was set up by the French Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs so that French institutions can attract foreign students to master’s and PhD programmes, in priority fields, as future decision-makers in the private and public sectors.

**Age**, as the official programme page states it: applicants from foreign countries up to **29 years old** at master’s level, and up to **35 years old** at PhD level. The 2027 application guide also measures age “at the moment of selection.” We are not repeating the birth-date cut-offs printed in that guide, because the copy we retrieved still refers to “sélection des candidatures 2026.” Ask the nominating institution, or read the current official documents, for the exact 2027 cut-off date.

**Nationality.** The guide says the programme is reserved for foreign nationals. Candidates who also hold French nationality are not eligible.

**Where you are studying now.** For the master’s track, candidates already studying in France are ineligible. For the doctoral track, candidates already studying in France are eligible, and students still studying abroad are given priority over those already living in France.

**What you would enrol in.** The student-facing page says institutions agree to enrol successful candidates in a course leading to a master’s degree, an engineering programme, or a joint doctoral programme (joint supervision and/or a dual degree) with a partner institution abroad. The guide adds that only state-accredited programmes conferring a master’s-level degree, including in engineering schools, are admitted. Programmes delivered abroad, distance programmes, and apprenticeship or professional-training contracts are not eligible.

**Fields of study**, as listed for both the master’s and doctoral components:

- Science and technology: biology and health; ecological transition; mathematics and digital; engineering sciences
- Humanities and social sciences: history, French language and civilization; law and political science; economics and management

## Duration and funding, in brief

The [English regulations](https://www.campusfrance.org/system/files/medias/documents/2026-10/reglementation_bourse_eiffel_2027_EN.pdf) and the student-facing page give these durations:

- Master’s: a maximum of **12 months** when enrolling in an M2, **24 months** when enrolling in an M1, and **36 months** for an engineering degree
- Doctorate: a period of up to 12 to 36 months, starting on 1 September of the award year, with the exact length depending on the year of enrolment, as the regulations spell out

Scholarship holders receive a monthly allowance of **€1,200** at master’s level and **€2,000** at doctoral level, both figures effective from 1 January 2026, plus services such as international and national transport, insurance, housing searches and cultural activities. **Tuition fees are not covered** by the Eiffel programme, except in the limited cases the regulations describe for French-government scholarship holders. Do not treat the monthly allowance as a full-cost award.

## Language is a side note

The 2027 application guide has a short language section. Institutions that preselect non-French-speaking candidates must make sure the candidate’s language level matches the level the programme requires. The file can include a language certificate or diploma “where applicable.” There is no single Eiffel-wide DELF, DALF, TCF or TOEFL score on that page.

The regulations also allow a short language course before the academic year starts: scholarship benefits can continue for a **maximum of one month, from August**, if the language training is mentioned in the application file. It cannot be added later. The **cost of the course itself is not included** in the scholarship; that cost stays the student’s responsibility.

If a programme is taught in French and you still need a diploma or a test for admission, [DELF vs TCF](/blog/delf-vs-tcf-which-french-test) and the [DELF exam guide](/exams/delf) are the places to sort out the certificate. That work only helps if a French institution has already agreed to present you. The deadline that matters this week is the nomination, not the exam date.
