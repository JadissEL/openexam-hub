**As of 27 November 2026.** The dates and certificates below come from [FH Aachen’s page for applicants with foreign proofs of education](https://www.fh-aachen.de/en/studies/applying/international-applicants/applicants-with-foreign-proofs-of-education), read on that day. They are a confirmed example, not a national rule. Other universities, and other programmes at the same university, set their own deadlines and language proofs. Check the page for the programme you want before you book an exam or pay a uni-assist fee.

This post does not repeat the wider comparison of German exams for visas, university admission and professional recognition. That sits in [German exams for visa, university or work](/blog/german-exams-visa-university-professional-recognition). What follows is the summer-semester window, as one university states it, and the German-language proofs that university names.

## There is no single national date

Germany does not publish one application deadline that covers every university for summer semester 2027. Some institutions use uni-assist, some do not, and master’s programmes often have their own calendars.

FH Aachen is explicit for bachelor applications submitted through uni-assist:

- **Winter semester:** 15 July
- **Summer semester:** 15 January

Documents uploaded after the deadline are not considered. The page also says uni-assist’s processing time is usually four to six weeks, so a file that only becomes complete on 15 January is already late in practical terms, even if it meets the formal date.

If you are aiming at a **summer semester 2027** start at FH Aachen, the date on that page is **15 January 2027**. If you are applying somewhere else, treat 15 January only as a date you might see — then read your own admissions page.

## What uni-assist does, at this university

FH Aachen describes uni-assist (the Service Center for International Applicants) as an association that works for many German universities. It checks whether your application is complete and whether you meet the admission requirements for the degree programme.

You apply via uni-assist for a bachelor’s degree at FH Aachen if you are a foreign applicant without a German university entrance qualification, including some cases where you have German citizenship but no German certificates, and including applicants who completed a Studienkolleg in Germany. You do **not** apply via uni-assist if, among other cases the page lists, you hold an upper-secondary certificate from the German-speaking Community in Belgium, you are a graduate of FH Aachen’s Freshman Institute, you are already studying at FH Aachen and changing programmes, or you are applying for a **master’s** degree. Master’s applications go through FH Aachen’s online portal, and those programmes have individual deadlines.

FH Aachen also says it does **not** use the VPD procedure. You upload a complete file to the My assist portal, including the service fee. If the review is positive, uni-assist forwards the application to the university automatically.

## German-language proofs at FH Aachen

Most degree programmes in Germany are taught in German, so a language certificate is often part of the file. FH Aachen splits the requirement into what you must already show **when you apply**, and what you must have **to enrol**.

**When applying via uni-assist**, the page says you must already have, and provide proof of, German at the level of:

- DSH-1, or
- Goethe-Zertifikat B2, or
- telc B2-Zertifikat, or
- TestDaF with 3 in all four parts (the page writes this as “TestDaF 4x3”), or
- ÖSD B2.

In addition, at the time of application, you must submit a confirmation of registration or a certificate of attendance for one of these higher examinations, or an equivalent:

- DSH-2
- Goethe-Zertifikat C2
- TestDaF with 4 in all four parts (“TestDaF 4x4”)
- telc Zertifikat C1 Hochschule
- ÖSD C2

**For registration (enrolment) in a German-language degree programme**, you must prove that you have passed:

- DSH-2, or
- TestDaF 4x4, or
- telc Zertifikat C1 Hochschule, or
- Goethe-Zertifikat C2, or
- ÖSD C2, or
- another equivalent certificate.

Those are **FH Aachen’s** words. They line up with the typical proofs described in [German exams for visa, university or work](/blog/german-exams-visa-university-professional-recognition) — TestDaF with TDN 4 in every part, DSH-2, Goethe-Zertifikat C2, telc Deutsch C1 Hochschule — but a university may accept a lower result for a particular programme. Do not treat this list as a national exam date, and do not assume another university wants the same two-step combination of a B2 certificate plus a booking for C1 or C2.

FH Aachen also flags exceptions on the same page:

- **IOS degree programmes** have lower German language requirements; the page tells you to note that, rather than spelling out every IOS score here.
- **Global Business and Economics** does not require proof of German.
- For **English-language degree programmes**, you provide proof of English instead.
- Some foreign school-leaving certificates can count as sufficient German, including examples the page gives from the German-speaking Community of Belgium, Luxembourg, and the French *Diplôme du Baccalauréat*. The page points to the Standing Conference of the Ministers of Education and Cultural Affairs for further information.

If your programme is taught in English, you still need to read the admissions page for the exact English test and score. [TOEFL vs IELTS](/blog/toefl-vs-ielts) is a starting point only; the university’s list is the one that counts.

## Check your programme, then book

A practical order, based only on what official pages actually say:

1. Confirm the **language of instruction** and the **accepted certificates** for your exact degree, including any lower or higher score the programme names.
2. Confirm the **application route** (uni-assist, a university portal, or both) and the **deadline**. At FH Aachen, bachelor applications with foreign certificates usually go through uni-assist by 15 January for the summer semester; master’s applications do not.
3. Book the exam that page names, with enough time for the result — or, if that university allows it, for a registration confirmation — to be in the file before the deadline.
