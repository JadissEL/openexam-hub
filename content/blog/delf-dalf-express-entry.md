A DELF B2 is a serious diploma, and it is easy to assume it will also work for [Express Entry](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html). It does not. This guide answers that question directly, then explains what a DELF or DALF is still good for, and how to move from a DELF B2 level toward an NCLC target on a test IRCC actually accepts.

**As of 27 November 2026.** Every rule below comes from an official page we read on that day. Rules change, so confirm anything that affects your file on the linked pages before you book or submit.

**Short answer.** IRCC lists two approved French tests for Express Entry: **TEF Canada** and **TCF Canada**. A **DELF** or **DALF** is not on that list, so you cannot enter it in an Express Entry profile. The diploma can still help for French citizenship, some French university applications, Quebec’s skilled-worker programme and Canadian citizenship. If you already have a DELF B2 and your goal is Express Entry, the next step is to sit TEF Canada or TCF Canada and aim at the NCLC level your programme asks for, often NCLC 7 in all four skills.

## What IRCC accepts for Express Entry

[IRCC’s language test results page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) is the list that matters. For French it names only:

- **TEF Canada** (Test d’évaluation de français)
- **TCF Canada** (Test de connaissance du français)

The English column lists CELPIP-General, IELTS General Training (not Academic, and not One Skill Retake) and PTE Core. There is no line for the DELF, the DALF, TCF Tout Public or a general TEF.

IRCC measures French with the **Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadien (NCLC)** and English with the **Canadian Language Benchmarks (CLB)**. Each skill is converted on its own. A strong result in one skill does not lift a weak one.

Results must be **less than two years old** when you complete your Express Entry profile **and** when you submit your application for permanent residence. If you apply with expired results, IRCC says it will refuse the application.

## Why a DELF B2 is not a substitute

The DELF and the DALF are diplomas. [France’s student site](https://www.etudiant.gouv.fr/fr/apprendre-et-certifier-son-niveau-de-francais-775) says a DELF is valid for life once obtained, and describes the DALF (C1 to C2) the same way. That is useful in France. It does not change IRCC’s list.

Two further points:

- **There is no official IRCC table that converts a DELF to NCLC.** The conversion tables on the language test page are only for the approved tests. We do not invent a “DELF B2 equals NCLC 7” figure here.
- **A lifetime diploma does not replace a two-year test result** for Express Entry. Even if your DELF is recent, IRCC still needs TEF Canada or TCF Canada scores.

For a wider comparison of the diploma and the TCF family, see [DELF vs TCF: which French test should you take?](/blog/delf-vs-tcf-which-french-test). To choose between the two tests IRCC does accept, see [TEF Canada vs TCF Canada](/blog/tef-canada-vs-tcf-canada).

## What a DELF or DALF is good for instead

![A table showing, for Express Entry, Canadian citizenship, Quebec skilled workers, French naturalisation and French university DAP applications, what each authority says about DELF or DALF and about TEF Canada or TCF Canada](/blog/delf-dalf-express-entry/where-each-is-accepted.webp)

*Sources: IRCC, quebec.ca, Service-Public and the French higher-education ministry, checked 27 November 2026. Chart by OpenExam Hub. Your own file may carry extra conditions.*

### Canadian citizenship

This is a different route from Express Entry. IRCC’s [citizenship language-proof page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/canadian-citizenship/become-canadian-citizen/eligibility/language-proof/step-5.html) accepts a **DELF at B1 or higher**, **any DALF result**, and **TEF Canada or TCF Canada** at **B1 or higher in listening and speaking**.

A diploma that cannot go in an Express Entry profile can still be used later, if you apply for Canadian citizenship and meet the other requirements on that page.

### Quebec skilled workers (PSTQ)

Quebec runs its own selection programme, separate from federal Express Entry. The [PSTQ French-proof page](https://www.quebec.ca/en/immigration/permanent/skilled-workers/skilled-worker-selection-program/requirements/knowledge-french) (last updated 25 November 2026 on the page) lists **DELF** and **DALF** from France Éducation international, alongside TCF-Québec, TCF Canada, TCF, TEFAQ, TEF Canada and TEF.

The same page says:

- results must be **two years old or less** on the date you apply for permanent selection;
- for DELF and DALF, a **full scan of the diploma** is required;
- only results issued by France Éducation international or the CCI Paris Île-de-France, taken at an approved centre, are accepted.

So a DELF can be used for this Quebec programme, but the two-year line still applies as the page is worded. If you hold an older diploma, check with the ministry before you rely on it. Other Quebec programmes have their own pages.

### French naturalisation

[Service-Public](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F11926) (page marked as verified on 1 January 2026) says that for naturalisation by decree, and for a declaration of nationality by marriage, you must show French at **B2 or above, spoken and written**. The accepted proofs include any diploma attesting at least B2 on the CEFR, a TCF attestation from France Éducation international issued less than two years ago, and a TEF attestation from the Paris chamber of commerce, also less than two years old.

A **DELF B2, DALF C1 or DALF C2** is a diploma at B2 or above, so it falls under that diploma line. Service-Public does not name the DELF in the list, so confirm with your prefecture if you want certainty. A **DELF B1 is below the bar**.

### French university admission (DAP)

For a first-year licence application through the **Demande d’admission préalable (DAP)**, the [2027–2028 ministry notice](https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2026-10/Notice%20dossier%20blanc%20DAP%202027-2028.pdf) asks for the **TCF Tout Public with a compulsory written expression test**, unless you are exempt. One exemption is holders of a French-language diploma from the higher-education ministry at **B2 or above**. [Campus France Tunisie](https://www.tunisie.campusfrance.org/faq/puis-je-etre-dispensee-du-tcf-dap) names the **DELF B2** and the **DALF C1 or C2** in its answer on who is exempt from the TCF for the DAP.

The notice’s file deadline is **15 December 2026**. Master’s and other programmes set their own rules, so check each university. France’s student site also notes that some programmes may ask for a DELF B2, and some for a DALF at C1 to C2. Country-by-country Campus France deadlines for Morocco, Tunisia and Egypt are in [Campus France 2027: which French test you need in Morocco, Tunisia and Egypt](/blog/campus-france-2027-french-test-maroc-tunisie-egypte).

## DELF B2 versus TEF Canada and TCF Canada

If Express Entry is the goal, the comparison is not “which diploma is better.” It is “which test can go in the profile.”

| | DELF / DALF | TEF Canada or TCF Canada |
|---|---|---|
| Listed by IRCC for Express Entry | No | Yes, these two only |
| What you receive | A diploma (DELF up to B2, DALF at C1 or C2) | Scores that IRCC converts to NCLC |
| How long it counts for Express Entry | It cannot be used | Results must be less than 2 years old at the profile and at the application |
| How long the document lasts in France | Valid for life once obtained, according to étudiant.gouv.fr | A TCF or TEF attestation is typically time-limited; check the version your authority names |
| Canadian citizenship | DELF B1 or higher; any DALF | Accepted at B1 or higher in listening and speaking |
| Quebec PSTQ | Listed, with a two-year rule on the page | Listed, with the same two-year rule |

Book the **Canada** version for Express Entry. TCF Tout Public and the general TEF are different tests, and they are not the names on IRCC’s Express Entry list.

## How to move from a DELF B2 level to an NCLC target

A DELF B2 shows that you passed a B2 exam. It does not give you an NCLC level, and it does not promise that you will score NCLC 7 on TEF Canada or TCF Canada. Those are different scales and different tests.

Still, if you already work at a B2 level, you are not starting from zero. The practical path is:

1. **Pick the NCLC you need, from IRCC’s pages, not from a conversion guess.**
2. **Book TEF Canada or TCF Canada**, then prepare for that test’s format.
3. **Enter the scores IRCC asks for**, and keep your results valid for the profile and the application.

### The NCLC target most people aim at

On the pages we checked on 27 November 2026, **NCLC 7 in all four abilities** is the figure that appears most often:

- **Federal Skilled Worker:** NCLC 7 in all four abilities is the minimum for French as your first official language. A second official language needs NCLC 5 (or CLB 5 in English).
- **Canadian Experience Class:** NCLC 7 in all four abilities for TEER 0 or 1 jobs, and NCLC 5 for TEER 2 or 3.
- **Federal Skilled Trades:** lower minima — NCLC 5 in speaking and listening, NCLC 4 in reading and writing.
- **French-language proficiency category:** IRCC lists this as a current category. To be eligible you need French test results of at least 7 in all four abilities, plus the requirements in each round’s [instructions](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/rounds-invitations/category-based-selection.html), and you must be eligible for one of the Express Entry programmes. Eligible candidates are then ranked by CRS score, so NCLC 7 is a condition, not a guarantee of an invitation.
- **CRS French bonus:** [IRCC’s CRS page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/check-score/crs-criteria.html) adds **25 points** for NCLC 7 or higher in all four French skills if your English is CLB 4 or lower (or you did not take an English test), or **50 points** if you also have CLB 5 or higher in all four English skills.

Meeting a programme minimum only makes you eligible. CRS points for a first official language also rise with each level above 7.

### Official NCLC 7 scores for TEF Canada and TCF Canada

![A table of NCLC 7 score bands for TEF Canada, using the old-score equivalence column, and for TCF Canada, in speaking, listening, reading and writing](/blog/delf-dalf-express-entry/nclc-7-tef-tcf.webp)

*Source: IRCC language test results page, checked 27 November 2026. Chart by OpenExam Hub. Not an official form.*

On [IRCC’s tables](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html):

| Skill | TEF Canada (Équivalence ancien score) | TCF Canada |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking | 310–348 | 10–11 (out of 20) |
| Listening | 249–279 | 458–502 |
| Reading | 207–232 | 453–498 |
| Writing | 310–348 | 10–11 (out of 20) |

For TEF Canada, IRCC says your language level is based on the **Équivalence ancien score** (previous score equivalency) column. Do not type the Score / 699 column into an Express Entry profile. Full tables, including other NCLC bands and the December 2023 to May 2024 TEF scoring note, are in [TEF Canada scores to CLB and NCLC](/blog/tef-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry) and [TCF Canada scores to CLB and NCLC](/blog/tcf-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry). For a side-by-side view of level 7, see [NCLC 7 / CLB 7: the TEF and TCF scores you need](/blog/nclc-7-clb-7-express-entry-scores).

You can also enter four scores in the [CLB and NCLC converter](/tools/clb-converter) (TEF Canada or TCF Canada tab). It applies IRCC’s published tables; it is not an official IRCC tool, and it does not replace confirming your figures on canada.ca.

### How to prepare

- Choose **TEF Canada or TCF Canada** using [the comparison guide](/blog/tef-canada-vs-tcf-canada). No official source ranks one as easier. Look at your nearest approved centre, your dates, and the official sample material for each test. Candidates in Morocco and Tunisia can start with the French guide to [TEF Canada centres, fees and free official prep](/blog/tef-canada-maroc-tunisie).
- Read the [TEF Canada exam guide](/exams/tef-canada) or the [TCF Canada exam guide](/exams/tcf-canada). If you still want the diploma explained in one place, see the [DELF exam guide](/exams/delf).
- Use official practice where it exists: the [TEF Canada 30-day study plan](/exams/tef-canada/30-day-study-plan) and the [TCF Canada official practice study path](/exams/tcf-canada/official-practice-study-path).
- Book early enough that your results will still be valid when you submit a permanent residence application, not only when you open a profile.
