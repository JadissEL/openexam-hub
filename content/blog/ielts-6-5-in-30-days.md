Nobody can promise you an IELTS 6.5, in 30 days or otherwise. Official IELTS pages do not say how long it takes to reach a band. A 30-day window can be useful if you are already close and want structured official practice. It is less realistic if you are starting much lower. This article explains what an overall 6.5 actually is, how it is rounded, and what official sources say it typically means. It is **not** a day-by-day plan. For that, use the [IELTS 8-week study plan](/exams/ielts/8-week-study-plan), which is built from official free materials. The [IELTS exam guide](/exams/ielts) covers format and official practice in more detail.

## What an overall 6.5 is

You receive a band for each of Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking, and an **overall** band. On the official [scoring-in-detail page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail), the overall score “is the average of the four section band scores rounded to the nearest half band.”

The same page is explicit about the rounding rule: if the average ends in **.25**, it is rounded **up** to the next half band; if it ends in **.75**, it is rounded **up** to the next whole band. Two official examples sit around the 6.5 line:

- 6.5, 6.5, 5.0 and 7.0 average **6.25**, reported as **6.5**.
- 6.5, 6.5, 5.5 and 6.0 average **6.125**, reported as **6.0**.

![Two official IELTS examples of overall-band rounding: 6.25 is rounded up to 6.5, and 6.125 is rounded to 6.0](/blog/ielts-6-5-in-30-days/overall-band-rounding.webp)

*Chart by OpenExam Hub. Source: IELTS, Scoring in detail, official examples A and C, checked 21 November 2026.*

That is why a single weaker skill can decide whether the overall band is 6.0 or 6.5 — and why a 30-day effort, if you use one at all, is often better spent on the paper that is pulling the average down than on a skill that is already at 6.5 or 7. Some organisations also set a **minimum in each skill**, not only an overall score. [IELTS tells recognising institutions](https://ielts.org/organisations/ielts-for-organisations/compare-ielts/ielts-and-the-cefr) to look at the band descriptors and to set the requirement that fits their course. Check yours before you book.

Listening and Reading are marked out of 40 and then converted to the nine-band scale. IELTS publishes **average** raw marks at whole bands and warns that the precise number “will vary slightly from test version to test version.” On that page, an average of 23 out of 40 in Listening, or in Academic Reading, sits at band 6; 30 sits at band 7. On General Training Reading the published averages are higher for the same bands (30 at band 6, 35 at band 7), because, as IELTS notes, it is usual that more questions must be right on the General Training Reading test to reach a given band.

Writing and Speaking are marked with published criteria. In Writing, Task 2 “carries more weight” than Task 1. The public [Speaking](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf) and [Writing](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf) band descriptors, linked from the official [resources for setting scores](https://ielts.org/organisations/ielts-for-organisations/understanding-ielts-scoring/resources-for-setting-your-ielts-scores), are the documents examiners use.

## What 6.5 typically means

The official skill-level paragraphs are written for **whole** bands. There is no separate “Band 6.5” description. An overall 6.5 therefore sits between [Band 6 (Competent)](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail) and [Band 7 (Good)](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail):

- **Band 6 — Competent.** “The test taker has an effective command of the language despite some inaccuracies, inappropriate usage and misunderstandings. They can use and understand reasonably complex language, particularly in familiar situations.”
- **Band 7 — Good.** “The test taker has operational command of the language, though with occasional inaccuracies, inappropriate usage and misunderstandings in some situations. They generally handle complex language well and understand detailed reasoning.”

On **CEFR**, official IELTS pages are careful. [IELTS and the CEFR](https://ielts.org/organisations/ielts-for-organisations/compare-ielts/ielts-and-the-cefr) says there “will not be a one-to-one correspondence between IELTS scores and CEFR levels,” and that the published figure is for overall bands, not a claim of exact equivalence. As a “very quick guide,” a later [IELTS article (17 November 2023)](https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/everything-you-need-to-know-about-ielts-and-the-cefr) says that if an organisation requires **5.5–6.5**, that is treated as **CEFR B2**, and that **7–8** correspond to **C1**. The CEFR page adds an important wrinkle for people targeting 6.5: official research “shows that a C1 minimum threshold would fall between the 6.5 and 7 bands… whilst many 6.5 test takers would be at C1, a number will be marginally below.” If an institution wants a high degree of confidence that an applicant is at C1, IELTS says it “may wish to set a requirement of 7, rather than 6.5.”

## Academic or General Training

A 6.5 is only useful if it is on the **module your organisation accepts**. [IELTS Academic](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types) is the usual test for university and higher education, and can also be used for professional registration. [IELTS General Training](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types) is aimed at migration to countries such as Australia, Canada, New Zealand and the UK, and at work or study below degree level. Speaking and Listening are the same on both; Reading and Writing differ. Always check the institution or government that will receive the result. For fees, dates and which test Express Entry accepts, see [IELTS fees, registration and 2027 test dates](/blog/ielts-fees-registration-2027-test-dates).

## Is 30 days realistic?

Official IELTS pages do not publish a preparation time for band 6.5. So any honest answer starts with your **current** scores, not a calendar.

The published descriptors are a better guide than a slogan. Moving from Band 5 (Modest: a “partial command,” “likely to make many mistakes”) to the Competent and Good levels quoted above is a change in what you can actually do in English, not only a change in exam technique. A 30-day window is more plausible if you already perform near 6.0 or 6.5 and need to steady a weak paper than if you are still at Band 5 across the board. That is a judgement, not a prediction, and it is not a guarantee.

If you do have about 30 days, use them on **official** practice rather than unofficial “6.5 in 30 days” score charts. Official [Academic](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/sample-test-questions/academic-test) and [General Training](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/sample-test-questions/general-training-test) sample tests on ielts.org are there so you can learn the format, sit tasks under timed conditions, and compare with model answers. The [British Council’s free practice tests](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/take-ielts/prepare/free-ielts-english-practice-tests) are another official source, including Academic and General Training papers. Read the public band descriptors against your own writing and speaking. That is preparation advice, not a day-by-day plan. The plan-shaped resource on this site is the [IELTS 8-week study plan](/exams/ielts/8-week-study-plan).

## Results and how long they last

On the official [results page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/getting-and-sharing-your-results), turnaround depends on the format: **IELTS on computer**, 1–5 days; **IELTS for UKVI**, 1–5 days; **IELTS Online**, 6–8 days. You receive a Test Report Form (paper or digital).

On **validity**, [IELTS recommends](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail) that results be treated as valid for **two years after the test**. Organisations may choose a longer period, so check the one that will see your score.
