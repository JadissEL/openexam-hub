On 5 November 2026, IRCC published the [2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/immigration-system/levels/supplementary-immigration-levels-2027-2029.html). The plan sets how many people Canada aims to admit as permanent residents, including a rising target for French-speaking admissions **outside Quebec**. This post reports those official figures, and the standing rule for the Express Entry French-language category. It does not forecast Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) scores or invitations to apply.

**As of 27 November 2026.** The numbers and the category rule below come from IRCC pages we read on that day. If a later levels plan or a Quebec plan updates them, the official table wins.

We already have detailed guides to the NCLC 7 score bands and to programme minimums: [NCLC 7 / CLB 7: the TEF and TCF scores you need](/blog/nclc-7-clb-7-express-entry-scores), [TEF Canada scores to NCLC](/blog/tef-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry) and [TCF Canada scores to NCLC](/blog/tcf-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry). This article does not repeat those tables.

## The official 2027–2029 figures

IRCC’s supplementary information page says overall permanent resident admissions **remain at 380,000** in each of 2027, 2028 and 2029, inside a range of **352,500 to 422,000**.

The same page “sets targets for French-speaking permanent resident admissions outside Quebec at 9.5% in 2027, 10.5% in 2028, and 12% in 2029.” The table next to that sentence gives the corresponding admission numbers:

![A table of official IRCC targets for French-speaking permanent resident admissions outside Quebec in 2027, 2028 and 2029](/blog/canada-2027-francophone-immigration-target-nclc-7/levels-targets.webp)

*Source: IRCC supplementary information for the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan, checked 27 November 2026. Chart by OpenExam Hub. Not an official form.*

| Year | Share outside Quebec | Planned admissions |
|---|---|---|
| 2027 | 9.5% | 31,825 |
| 2028 | 10.5% | 35,175 |
| 2029 | 12% | 40,200 |

A footnote on that table matters. IRCC says these figures “represent the number of admissions required to meet” those percentage targets “of overall permanent resident admissions,” and that they are “calculated based on how many admissions within the Levels Plan would be necessary to meet the targets, based on the overall ranges for admissions outside of Quebec.” It also says that **once Quebec tables its 2027 immigration levels plan, these targets may require updates**.

In other words, the 9.5% and 31,825 figures are official targets for admissions **outside Quebec**, not a promise of how many invitations any one Express Entry draw will issue, and they may be revised after Quebec’s plan is tabled. IRCC noted that Quebec’s plan was to be tabled in fall 2026; we have not treated a later Quebec document as confirmed here.

## The French-language Express Entry category still uses NCLC 7

The levels plan is a target for admissions. The rule that decides who can be invited in a French-language category round is published separately, on IRCC’s [category-based selection page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/rounds-invitations/category-based-selection.html).

When we read that page on 27 November 2026, **French-language proficiency** was listed as a current category. To be eligible, IRCC says you must:

- have French-language test results that show a **minimum score of 7 in all four language abilities** on the NCLC;
- meet all of the requirements in the instructions for that round.

You must also be eligible for one of the Express Entry programmes (Federal Skilled Worker, Federal Skilled Trades or Canadian Experience Class). Eligible candidates are then ranked, and category rounds still use CRS scores to decide who is invited. NCLC 7 is a condition for the category, not a guarantee of an invitation. IRCC does not publish a calendar of French-category draws, and this post does not invent one.

The only French tests on [IRCC’s Express Entry list](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) are **TEF Canada** and **TCF Canada**. A [DELF or DALF cannot go in an Express Entry profile](/blog/delf-dalf-express-entry). Results must be less than two years old when you complete your profile and when you apply for permanent residence.

## What this means if you are preparing a French test

If you are aiming at the French-language category, the official benchmark to plan around is **NCLC 7 in speaking, listening, reading and writing**, on TEF Canada or TCF Canada. For the official score bands, use:

- [NCLC 7 / CLB 7: the TEF and TCF scores you need](/blog/nclc-7-clb-7-express-entry-scores)
- [TEF Canada scores to CLB and NCLC](/blog/tef-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry)
- [TCF Canada scores to CLB and NCLC](/blog/tcf-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry)
- the [CLB and NCLC converter](/tools/clb-converter), which applies IRCC’s published tables and is not an official IRCC tool

To choose between the two approved tests, see [TEF Canada vs TCF Canada](/blog/tef-canada-vs-tcf-canada).

## What we could not verify

We do not have a confirmed 2027 Quebec immigration levels plan on an official page, so we have not added a Quebec target. IRCC itself says the outside-Quebec figures may need updating once that plan is tabled.

We also do not report unofficial CRS cut-offs or invitation counts, and we do not guess when the next French-language category round will take place. Those figures are not in the levels-plan table or on the category page we checked.
