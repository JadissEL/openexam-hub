If you are going to Canada through a French test, you will see two names again and again: **TEF Canada** and **TCF Canada**. This guide compares them using only official pages from IRCC, the Quebec government and the exam bodies. We do not tell you one is easier, because no official source does. Rules change, so confirm anything that affects your file on the linked pages before you book.

## The short answer

On the pages we checked on 26 November 2026, **both tests are listed for all three routes**: Express Entry, Canadian citizenship and Quebec’s skilled-worker programme (PSTQ). For those routes, the choice is rarely about which test is accepted. It is more about which test fits your goal, your nearest approved centre and your dates.

## Express Entry

[IRCC’s accepted-tests page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) names two French tests: **TEF Canada** and **TCF Canada**. It does not state a preference. Programme minimums are set by the programme, not the test. For example, the Federal Skilled Worker first-language minimum is NCLC 7 in all four abilities, and the Canadian Experience Class minimum is NCLC 7 for TEER 0 or 1 jobs and NCLC 5 for TEER 2 or 3.

IRCC also says results must be **less than two years old** when you complete your profile and when you submit your application. Expired results at application lead to refusal.

If you want the conversion tables, we have one guide for each test: [TEF Canada scores to CLB and NCLC](/blog/tef-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry) and [TCF Canada scores to CLB and NCLC](/blog/tcf-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry). For the most common target, see [NCLC 7 / CLB 7: the TEF Canada and TCF Canada scores you need](/blog/nclc-7-clb-7-express-entry-scores). There is also a [CLB and NCLC converter](/tools/clb-converter) with TEF Canada and TCF Canada tabs (and IELTS General Training and CELPIP-General tabs) that applies IRCC’s official tables to your four scores, shows whether you meet NCLC 7 or 9, and lists first-official-language CRS points.

## Quebec

Quebec selects skilled workers through its own programme, the PSTQ. On the [PSTQ French-proof page](https://www.quebec.ca/immigration/permanente/travailleurs-qualifies/programme-selection-travailleurs-qualifies/exigences/connaissance-francais) (last updated 25 November 2026 on the page), the ministry lists **TCF-Canada and TEF Canada** among its accepted tests. The list also includes TCF-Québec, TCF, TEFAQ, TEF, DELF and DALF. Three points from that page:

- Results must be **two years old or less** on the date you submit your application for permanent selection.
- Only results issued by the test providers (France Éducation international and the CCI Paris Île-de-France) are accepted.
- You must take the test at a centre approved by the provider.

Quebec converts results to its own scale, the *Échelle québécoise des niveaux de compétence en français*, and the page links its conversion tables. This guide covers only the PSTQ French-proof page. If you apply under another Quebec programme, read that programme’s own page.

## Canadian citizenship

IRCC asks applicants aged 18 to 54 to prove speaking and listening at CLB 4 or higher. On its [citizenship language-proof page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/canadian-citizenship/become-canadian-citizen/eligibility/language-proof/step-5.html), both **TEF Canada** and **TCF Canada** are accepted at **B1 or higher in listening and speaking**. IRCC says to include a copy of your results even if they have expired.

For this route you do not need all four skills. Le français des affaires says that for citizenship, TEF Canada requires only the two compulsory tests, listening and oral expression, and that all the tests must be taken on the same day for the certificate to be recognised by the Canadian authorities. [Check France Éducation international’s page](https://www.france-education-international.fr/test/tcf-canada) for the TCF Canada equivalent.

## What actually differs

![Table comparing TEF Canada and TCF Canada: who runs them, which scores to type into Express Entry, the NCLC 7 listening band, and result validity](/blog/tef-canada-vs-tcf-canada/what-differs.webp)

*Chart by OpenExam Hub. Sources: IRCC language test results page and Le français des affaires, checked 26 November 2026.*

- **Who runs the test.** TEF Canada is run by the CCI Paris Île-de-France through [Le français des affaires](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/en/candidate/test-evaluation-francais/tef-canada/presentation/). TCF Canada is run by [France Éducation international](https://www.france-education-international.fr/test/tcf-canada). They are separate bodies with separate booking systems and certificates.
- **The numbers you enter.** For TEF Canada, IRCC’s Express Entry table uses the *old-score equivalence* column on your certificate, **not** the Score / 699 column. For TCF Canada, IRCC’s table scores speaking and writing out of 20, and listening and reading out of 699. The same NCLC level therefore appears as different numbers: NCLC 7 in listening is 249–279 for TEF Canada and 458–502 for TCF Canada on IRCC’s table.
- **TEF Canada format.** For an immigration application, Le français des affaires lists four tests: reading (40 multiple-choice questions, 1 hour), listening (40 multiple-choice questions, 40 minutes), written expression (2 sections, 1 hour, 80 and 200 words minimum) and oral expression (2 sections, 15 minutes in total). Our [TEF Canada exam guide](/exams/tef-canada) summarises this.
- **TCF Canada format.** France Éducation international’s page blocked our automated check on 26 November 2026, so we are not summarising its format or timings here. Read it on the [official page](https://www.france-education-international.fr/test/tcf-canada), and see our [TCF Canada exam guide](/exams/tcf-canada).

What does **not** differ: for Express Entry, IRCC converts both to the same NCLC levels, judges each skill separately, and counts results as valid for under two years. A strong skill does not lift a weak one.

## How to choose

1. **Start from the rule for your route.** Express Entry, citizenship and PSTQ list both, so the rule can only tell you the level you need, not the brand.
2. **Pick the Canada version for Express Entry.** IRCC’s Express Entry page names TEF Canada and TCF Canada only, so do not book TCF tout public or the general TEF for that route. Citizenship and Quebec list more versions, but both still name TCF Canada and TEF Canada.
3. **Look at your centre and dates.** Seats, dates and fees are set by each approved centre. We cannot give you a global price. Check your nearest centre’s own page and book the first slot that leaves you time to prepare.
4. **Try the official sample material.** Each exam body publishes its own. Use it to see which style suits you. That is a personal call, not a ranking.
5. **Do not swap mid-way without a reason.** If you already hold a valid result at the level you need, a second test is usually only worth it if one skill sits below the level you need.

Whichever you choose, convert your scores with IRCC’s tables and keep your certificate. Never type numbers from one test’s table for the other.
