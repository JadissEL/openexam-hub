If you are choosing between **TOEFL iBT** and **IELTS**, start with one question: who will read your score? A university, an immigration authority or a professional body decides which tests it accepts. After that, the two tests differ in format, scoring and how you can take them. This guide covers both steps, using ETS pages, IELTS pages and IRCC, all checked on 27 November 2026.

**The short version:**

- **Applying for Canadian Express Entry?** IRCC does not list TOEFL. Its English options are IELTS General Training, CELPIP-General and PTE Core.
- **Applying to a university?** Use the university’s own list. If it accepts both tests, the choice comes down to format and how you like to test.
- **Applying for a visa or professional registration?** Check the exact test and version the authority names before you book.

## What changed in TOEFL iBT in January 2026

Many older articles about TOEFL describe the previous test. Two things changed, according to ETS.

**The scale.** ETS says that as of 21 January 2026, TOEFL iBT score reports use a **1–6 scale in half-point steps**, aligned with the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR). You get four section scores and an overall score. The overall is the average of the four sections, rounded to the nearest half band. For a two-year transition after January 2026, you also get a comparable overall score on the old 0–120 scale. ETS says it will show three things on every score report during that period: a CEFR level, the 1–6 scores and the comparable 0–120 total. Some universities have not yet updated their requirements, so ETS tells test takers that both formats are on the official report. Details are on [ETS’s scores page](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/scores/understand-scores.html).

**The test itself.** ETS’s [test content page](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/about/content.html) lists the sections and task types:

| Section | Task types ETS lists | Items (approx.) |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Reading | Complete the Words; Read in Daily Life; Read an Academic Passage | 50 |
| Listening | Listen and Choose a Response; Listen to a Conversation; Listen to an Announcement; Listen to an Academic Talk | 47 |
| Writing | Build a Sentence; Write an Email; Write for an Academic Discussion | 12 |
| Speaking | Listen and Repeat; Take an Interview | 11 |

ETS says the test adapts, so the number of items and the time can vary, and that you should allow about two hours. It also says there are no scheduled breaks. [OpenExam Hub’s TOEFL guide](/exams/toefl) summarises the format and links to ETS’s free official practice.

## IELTS in brief

IELTS comes in more than one version. On [ielts.org’s test types page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types), IELTS Academic is for study and professional registration, IELTS General Training is for migration, work experience and training, and IELTS for UKVI is for UK visa purposes. Listening and Speaking are the same in Academic and General Training, while Reading and Writing differ.

For IELTS Academic, ielts.org gives a test time of **2 hours 45 minutes**: Listening about 30 minutes, Reading 60 minutes, Writing 60 minutes and Speaking 11–14 minutes. Listening and Reading have 40 questions each, per [IELTS’s scoring page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail). Scores are band scores from 0 to 9, and the overall band is the average of the four sections, rounded to the nearest half band. IELTS says its Speaking test is assessed by a real person.

TOEFL has no separate General Training version in ETS’s pages that we reviewed, so the module question you face with IELTS does not arise. For the IELTS module question, see [IELTS Academic or General Training: which one you need for Express Entry](/blog/ielts-for-express-entry-academic-or-general).

## Express Entry: TOEFL is not on IRCC’s list

For immigration to Canada through Express Entry, the answer is clear. On its [language test results page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html), IRCC lists these approved tests:

- **English:** CELPIP (you must take CELPIP-General), IELTS (you must take the General Training option; IRCC does not accept IELTS One Skill Retake) and PTE Core.
- **French:** TEF Canada and TCF Canada.

TOEFL does not appear on that list, so do not plan to use a TOEFL result for Express Entry. This applies to Express Entry only. Other Canadian processes, such as a study permit or a university offer, have their own rules, so check the page for what you are applying for.

If you are aiming for Canadian permanent residence, the useful IELTS reading is the General Training table. It is covered in [IELTS to CLB for Express Entry](/blog/ielts-to-clb-express-entry).

## University admission: let the university decide

Universities publish their own English-language requirements, and ETS says as much: there are no passing or failing TOEFL scores set by ETS, and each institution sets its own. During the scale change, you may see a university list its requirement as 0–120 or as 1–6. ETS says the official report carries both formats, so you do not need to convert anything yourself.

Both providers describe wide acceptance. ETS says more than 13,500 institutions in over 160 countries accept TOEFL scores, and IELTS says over 13,500 institutions accept IELTS. These are the providers’ own figures, and the only list that matters is your university’s. Look for three things on its page: which tests it accepts, the minimum overall score, and any minimum for each section.

## Can you convert between TOEFL and IELTS?

Be careful here. ETS publishes a comparison table for institutions, for tests taken on or after 21 January 2026. This table uses the 1–6 scale:

*Chart by OpenExam Hub, from ETS’s TOEFL score scale update guide for institutions, checked 27 November 2026. Not an official ETS graphic. ETS’s full table also lists section scores.*

ETS says its totals are the average of the section scores, rounded up to the next half band, to minimise false positives, and that the totals can be reached with different combinations of section scores.

IELTS takes a different view. In a brochure on ielts.org, dated May 2026, IELTS says the 2025 alignment between TOEFL and IELTS Academic is “obsolete” for the new TOEFL, and recommends avoiding “direct equivalency assumptions” between old TOEFL, new TOEFL and IELTS Academic scores. It also says new TOEFL reading passages are about 60% shorter and that the speaking and writing components are about 50% shorter. These are IELTS’s claims about a competitor’s test. We have not independently checked them, and ETS describes the test differently: on ETS’s [why-choose page](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/about/why.html), it says TOEFL iBT measures all four academic English skills the way they are used in a classroom.

For how IELTS bands map to CEFR levels, see [IELTS to CEFR](/blog/ielts-to-cefr).

What this means for you: both organisations have a commercial interest, and they disagree. Do not rely on a conversion you found online. Ask the university or authority which scores it accepts, and book the test it names.

## How to choose, once your receiving organisation accepts both

If your university or employer lists both tests, these are reasonable tie-breakers. They are our suggestions based on the facts above, not official advice:

- **Time and format.** ETS asks you to allow about two hours with no scheduled breaks. IELTS Academic is 2 hours 45 minutes. If you prefer a shorter sitting, that favours TOEFL. If you prefer longer Reading and Writing tasks, IELTS’s timings are the longer ones.
- **Speaking.** IELTS says a real person assesses Speaking. TOEFL’s Speaking is two task types (Listen and Repeat, Take an Interview), scored centrally. Decide which suits you, and try a free official sample of each.
- **Where and how you can test.** TOEFL iBT Home Edition is available worldwide, per ETS. IELTS Online is limited to Academic and is not for visas or immigration. Check what is open near you.
- **Practice material.** Try the official sample tasks for both before you pay. [OpenExam Hub’s TOEFL page](/exams/toefl) and [IELTS page](/exams/ielts) link to the free official material.
- **Cost.** ETS’s fee depends on the testing location, so we show no figure. See [ETS’s fee page](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/register/fees.html), and for IELTS see [IELTS fees, registration and 2027 test dates](/blog/ielts-fees-registration-2027-test-dates).
