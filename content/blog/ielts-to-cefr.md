IELTS reports results as bands from 0 to 9. The CEFR, the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages, describes six levels from A1 to C2. People search for “IELTS to CEFR” because a university, employer or visa page names a CEFR level and they want to know which IELTS result that means. This post reads the official pages, checked on 27 November 2026. It gives an approximate alignment, not a guarantee, and it is not an instruction from any institution. Always check the level and score your institution or visa route actually names.

## The short answer

IELTS.org maps **overall** IELTS bands to CEFR levels roughly like this. The borders are on 5.0, 6.5 and 8.0, so those bands can fall on either side.

![Chart of overall IELTS bands 4.0 to 9.0 against CEFR levels B1, B2, C1 and C2, with 5.0, 6.5 and 8.0 shown as borderline between two levels, and a note that IELTS plots no band on A1 or A2](/blog/ielts-to-cefr/ielts-band-to-cefr-chart.webp)

*Chart by OpenExam Hub, from IELTS.org’s “IELTS and the CEFR” page and its figure in the IELTS scores guide, checked 27 November 2026. Not an official IELTS graphic.*

| CEFR level | Overall IELTS band, per IELTS’s figure | Borderline bands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C2 | 8.5–9.0 | 8.0 (C1 or C2) |
| C1 | 7.0–7.5 | 6.5 (B2 or C1), 8.0 |
| B2 | 5.5–6.0 | 5.0 (B1 or B2), 6.5 |
| B1 | 4.0–4.5 | 5.0 |
| A2, A1 | no band plotted | — |

IELTS states that [band 8.5 and higher is recognised as C2 and band 8 is borderline](https://ielts.org/organisations/ielts-for-organisations/compare-ielts/ielts-and-the-cefr). The other rows are read off IELTS’s published figure, where bands 4 and 4.5 sit in B1, 5.5 and 6 in B2, and 7 and 7.5 in C1.

## Why the official pages look slightly different

You will see two simpler versions, and they differ from the figure only on the border bands:

- The British Council’s [IELTS page](https://www.britishcouncil.gr/en/exam/ielts/about) shows a table: below 4.0 is “Below B1”, 4.0–5.0 is B1, 5.5–6.5 is B2, 7.0–8.0 is C1 and 8.5–9.0 is C2.
- IELTS’s 2023 article calls this a “very quick guide”: [5.5–6.5 is B2 and 7–8 corresponds to C1](https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/everything-you-need-to-know-about-ielts-and-the-cefr), pointing to the full diagram for the complete picture.

Those tables put the border bands (5.0, 6.5, 8.0) in one level. The figure shows them as borderline. Neither is wrong; the second is the more careful one. IELTS explains why: “IELTS band scores have never aligned exactly with the CEFR transition points.”

## Why it is only approximate

IELTS.org is direct about the limits of this mapping:

- IELTS “is not a level-based test, but rather designed to span a much broader proficiency continuum,” so “there will not be a one-to-one correspondence between IELTS scores and CEFR levels.”
- The figure refers to **overall** band scores, “not the individual band scores for Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking,” and should not be read as a claim of exact equivalence.
- A C1 minimum “would fall between the 6.5 and 7 bands.” Many people at 6.5 would be at C1, but “a number will be marginally below.” An institution that needs high confidence at C1 may set 7 rather than 6.5.

It follows that a band is not a promise of a level, and the same overall band can hide very different skill profiles. [IELTS 6.5 in 30 days](/blog/ielts-6-5-in-30-days) explains how the overall band is rounded from the four skills.

## What the CEFR levels describe

The [Council of Europe’s global scale](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale) describes the levels in terms of what a learner can do. In short:

- **B1** can understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters and produce simple connected text.
- **B2** can understand the main ideas of complex text and produce clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects.
- **C1** can understand a wide range of demanding, longer texts and use language flexibly for social, academic and professional purposes.
- **C2** can understand with ease virtually everything heard or read.
- **A2** and **A1** are the basic-user levels, for simple, routine exchanges. IELTS’s figure does not plot a band there.

## UK visas: what the Home Office lists per skill

For UK visa and citizenship applications, the Home Office states that the [CEFR level you need depends on your route](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt). Its approved-test lists give the minimum grade for each level. For **IELTS for UKVI**, the four-skills test, the lists show:

![Home Office minimums for IELTS for UKVI: 4.0 in each skill for B1, 5.5 for B2, 7.0 for C1 and 8.5 for C2](/blog/ielts-to-cefr/ukvi-ielts-minimums-by-cefr.webp)

*Chart by OpenExam Hub, from the GOV.UK lists of approved secure English language tests (UK and outside the UK, same IELTS rows), checked 27 November 2026.*

These are **per-skill** minimums: Listening, Speaking, Reading and Writing each need to reach the figure. They are a route requirement, not a general conversion. For A1, A2 and B1 on the speaking-and-listening-only routes, the lists show **IELTS Life Skills** with a grade of “Pass,” not 9-band scores.

Two more points from the same pages. Test results are valid for two years from the date the test is awarded, and you must take the test version the Home Office approves: for IELTS, “IELTS for UKVI” or “IELTS Life Skills.” Make sure the test you book is the approved one.

Rules also set CEFR levels in other places. For example, the [Immigration Rules Appendix English Language](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-english-language) allows a student on a degree-level course, sponsored by a higher education provider with a track record of compliance, to meet the requirement if the Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies states that the provider assessed their English as equivalent to B2 or above in all four components (reading, writing, speaking and listening). Read the route page for your own situation; this post cannot say which level you need.

## How to use this when you are planning

1. **Find the exact requirement.** If a page names a CEFR level, look for the IELTS score the institution itself lists. If it only gives a CEFR level, ask the institution which IELTS band and which per-skill minimum it accepts.
2. **Do not treat a border band as safe.** If your target is exactly 5.0, 6.5 or 8.0, ask whether the institution is happy with that band for the level it names.
3. **Check each skill.** An overall band can be reached with one weaker skill. Many requirements, including the UK ones above, apply per skill.
4. **Canada is a different table.** For Express Entry, IRCC converts each skill with its own table, not the CEFR. See [IELTS to CLB for Express Entry](/blog/ielts-to-clb-express-entry), and [which IELTS module Express Entry needs](/blog/ielts-for-express-entry-academic-or-general).
5. **Check dates and fees separately.** [IELTS fees, registration and 2027 test dates](/blog/ielts-fees-registration-2027-test-dates) covers what to book, and the [IELTS exam guide](/exams/ielts) covers the format and official free practice.
