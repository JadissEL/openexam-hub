For Express Entry, Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) measures English in **Canadian Language Benchmarks (CLB)**, one level for each skill. If you took **CELPIP-General**, the conversion is easy: on IRCC’s table, each CELPIP level sits on the same CLB level. This guide shows that table as IRCC publishes it, the minimum level each program asks for, and a few CELPIP rules that matter for an application. It was checked on 27 November 2026 against [IRCC’s language-test page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) and the official [CELPIP](https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/) pages. It is not immigration advice and not a promise about your CRS score or an invitation.

## Which CELPIP test IRCC accepts

IRCC’s list of approved tests includes **CELPIP**, with this instruction: “You must take the CELPIP-General test.” The other English options on that page are IELTS General Training and PTE Core. For French, IRCC lists TEF Canada and TCF Canada.

CELPIP has two versions, according to [the CELPIP test overview](https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/):

- **CELPIP-General** assesses Speaking, Listening, Reading and Writing, takes under 2 hours 50 minutes in one sitting, and is described as used for permanent residence applications, professional designation, and by universities, colleges and vocational programs.
- **CELPIP-General LS** assesses only Speaking and Listening, takes about 70 minutes, and is described as the IRCC-approved test for Canadian citizenship applications.

An LS result only has two skills, so it cannot give IRCC the four levels Express Entry asks for. Book CELPIP-General, and check which version any other organisation names before you register.

## IRCC’s CELPIP table, as published

IRCC prints the CELPIP table under each program. Under the **Federal Skilled Worker Program**, the table for the first official language reads:

| CLB Level | Speaking | Listening | Reading | Writing | Points per ability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 CELPIP-G | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 4 |
| 8 CELPIP-G | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 5 |
| 9 CELPIP-G | 9 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 6 |
| 10 and above CELPIP-G | 10+ | 10+ | 10+ | 10+ | 6 |

IRCC labels that block “First official language (maximum 24 points)”. The points shown are the Federal Skilled Worker selection points for each ability, not Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points. The same page adds the second official language rule: **4 points in total** if you meet CLB 5 or above in all four abilities (CELPIP 5–12 in each).

Under the **Federal Skilled Trades Program**, IRCC lists a wider range of levels:

| CLB Level | Speaking | Listening | Reading | Writing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 and above | 9+ | 9+ | 9+ | 9+ |
| 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| 7 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7 |
| 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |

Under the **Canadian Experience Class**, the table adds the job’s NOC TEER group:

| NOC TEER | CLB Level | Speaking | Listening | Reading | Writing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 and 1 | 9 and above | 9+ | 9+ | 9+ | 9+ |
| 0 and 1 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| 0 and 1 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7 |
| 2 and 3 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| 2 and 3 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 |

**How to read a result.** Use your four CELPIP levels one at a time. Each level matches the same CLB level. CELPIP itself says its scores are calibrated against the CLB, and its [results page](https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/test-results/) shows CELPIP 7 as CLB 7 and CEFR B2. CELPIP reports levels from 0 to 12. IRCC’s table stops at “10 and above”, so anything higher than 10 sits in that top row.

## The minimum CELPIP level for each program

IRCC sets the English minimum by program, and it applies to **each ability**:

| Program | Minimum CLB (English) |
| --- | --- |
| Federal Skilled Worker, first official language | CLB 7 in all four abilities |
| Canadian Experience Class, TEER 0 or 1 | CLB 7 in all four abilities |
| Canadian Experience Class, TEER 2 or 3 | CLB 5 in all four abilities |
| Federal Skilled Trades | CLB 5 in speaking and listening; CLB 4 in reading and writing |

Because CELPIP levels match CLB levels, that means CELPIP 7 in each skill for Federal Skilled Worker, or for Canadian Experience Class in TEER 0 or 1.

![IRCC minimums by program, shown as the CELPIP-General level needed in each skill: 7 for Federal Skilled Worker and CEC TEER 0 or 1, 5 for CEC TEER 2 or 3, and 5 for speaking and listening plus 4 for reading and writing for Federal Skilled Trades](/blog/celpip-to-clb-express-entry/minimum-celpip-levels-by-program.webp)

*Chart by OpenExam Hub, from IRCC’s Express Entry language test results page, checked 27 November 2026.*

A high level in one skill does not make up for a low one. With Speaking 9, Listening 8, Reading 6 and Writing 7, your lowest skill is CLB 6, so you do not meet the CLB 7 minimum for Federal Skilled Worker. With Speaking 9, Listening 8, Reading 7 and Writing 7, you do meet it, and IRCC’s Federal Skilled Worker table gives 6 + 5 + 4 + 4 = 19 of the 24 first-language points. Always confirm figures for your own profile on IRCC’s page. For why level 7 comes up so often, see [NCLC 7 / CLB 7: what TEF Canada and TCF Canada scores you need](/blog/nclc-7-clb-7-express-entry-scores). That post covers the French tests.

## How long CELPIP results last

Two rules apply, and for Express Entry IRCC’s rule is the one to follow.

- **IRCC:** your results “must be less than 2 years old” when you complete your Express Entry profile and when you submit your application for permanent residence. If you apply with expired results, IRCC says it will refuse your application. If your results will expire while you wait, IRCC says to be tested again, apply before they expire, or decline an invitation and go back into the pool.
- **CELPIP:** scores are valid for two years from the date your score report is issued.

On the CELPIP results page, the official score report is available online in your CELPIP account after the test, as a PDF you download to submit to an organisation such as IRCC. IRCC’s page also says to enter your results into your profile, and, if the system asks, the language test result form or certificate number and test PIN.

## If you need a re-evaluation

CELPIP says you can apply for a re-evaluation of some or all components of the CELPIP-General test within six months of the test date. You pay a fee when you apply, and the fee for a component is refunded if its level changes. There is a limit of one re-evaluation for each component. CELPIP also notes that re-evaluating Listening or Reading is unlikely to change your score, because those two components are computer rated. Read the full terms on the [CELPIP results page](https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/test-results/) before you apply.
