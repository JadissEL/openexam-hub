If you are booking IELTS for **Express Entry** or another Canada permanent residence programme, the test you need is **IELTS General Training**, not Academic. That is the option [IRCC lists](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) on its Express Entry language-test page, checked on 26 November 2026.

IELTS Academic is the usual test for university admission and, on IELTS’s own pages, for professional registration. An Academic Test Report Form does not become a General Training result. If a university or a regulator wants Academic, and IRCC wants General Training, you have to meet each organisation’s rule — not assume one sitting will cover both.

This page is about **which module to book**. It is not a score table, a study plan, or a promise about your band.

## Express Entry requires General Training

IRCC’s approved English tests for Express Entry are CELPIP-General, **IELTS General Training**, and PTE Core. On IELTS, the page is explicit: “You must take the IELTS General Training option.” Academic is not in that list.

The same IRCC page also says: “We don’t accept IELTS One Skill Retake for Express Entry.”

[IELTS](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types) describes General Training as the test for people who wish to migrate to a country where English is spoken, including Canada, and for training or study below degree level. That is consistent with IRCC’s instruction, but it is IRCC — not the test board — that decides which result it will accept for a Canadian immigration application.

### One Skill Retake and IELTS Online are not a workaround

**One Skill Retake** is an official IELTS product. On the [One Skill Retake page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/booking-your-test/one-skill-retake), you can retake a single skill after a full **IELTS on computer** test, once, within 60 days, where the centre offers it. IELTS tells you to check with the receiving organisation before you book. For Express Entry, IRCC has already answered: it does not accept those results. UKVI accepting One Skill Retake in selected locations does not change the Canadian rule.

**IELTS Online** is also easy to confuse with a computer-delivered test in a centre. They are not the same thing.

- [ielts.org](https://ielts.org/take-a-test) lists two options side by side. In person, on a computer at an IELTS test centre: “Visa applicants must test in person.” Online (taken remotely): “Not accepted by visa immigration authorities.”
- The [IELTS Online page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-online) describes it as the **online Academic** test, taken from anywhere, with the same content as Academic in a centre. It adds: “For visa and immigration purposes, you should continue to take your test in an authorised IELTS test centre.”
- The official [Guide for Test Takers](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-downloadable-assets/ielts-guidance-and-support/ielts-guides/ielts-guide-for-test-takers.pdf) says IELTS Online is “IELTS Academic test only.”

So for Express Entry you want **General Training, in person, at an authorised test centre**. Sitting General Training **on computer in that centre** is still an in-person test. It is not IELTS Online.

### Other Canada permanent residence programmes

IRCC’s [help centre answer](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=638) on whether you need a language test to immigrate to Canada also says, for English, “You need to take the IELTS General Training test.” That answer lists several economic programmes, including Express Entry, the Atlantic Immigration Program and the Start-up visa program. It is still worth opening the language-test page for the programme you are using, because rules can differ.

Results must be less than **two years old** when you complete your Express Entry profile **and** when you submit your permanent residence application. If you apply with expired results, IRCC says it will refuse the application.

IRCC converts General Training scores to **CLB** levels and publishes the official bands on the same [language test results](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) page. This article does not repeat that table.

## When Academic is the test you need

Book **IELTS Academic** when the organisation that will read your results is a university or, in most professional-registration cases, a regulator — not IRCC.

[IELTS’s test-types page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types) says Academic is likely the right choice if you plan to study in a country where English is spoken. It “assesses if you are ready to begin studying in English” and “features vocabulary that is familiar within an academic setting.” You can also take Academic “for professional registration purposes.” The lists on that page are: university, higher education, and professional registration.

The [Academic test page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test) is more specific: choose Academic “if you wish to study at undergraduate or postgraduate levels, or if you are seeking professional registration, e.g. doctors and nurses.” You can take Academic at a test centre or remotely online (with the visa caveat above).

The official [Guide for Test Takers](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-downloadable-assets/ielts-guidance-and-support/ielts-guides/ielts-guide-for-test-takers.pdf) puts this in a table. Academic is marked **Yes** for undergraduate or postgraduate study and for registering with a professional body; General Training is marked **No** for both of those. For training or study below degree level, and for migrating to an English-speaking country, the guide marks **Yes** for both tests — then tells you to check directly with the institution or organisation. For Canadian Express Entry, that organisation is IRCC, and it requires General Training.

Always confirm the test type and the score with the university or the professional body before you pay. IELTS itself says you should check entry requirements for your target country, institution or association.

## How the two tests differ

You do not need a different listening or speaking paper. [ielts.org](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types) says the **Speaking and Listening** sections are the same in Academic and General Training. **Reading and Writing** are not.

The official guide sets the differences out as follows (both tests last 2 hours and 45 minutes in total):

- **Academic Reading** (60 minutes, 40 questions): three sections, each with one long passage of general interest to undergraduate or postgraduate students, possibly with diagrams. Sources can include books, journals and newspapers.
- **General Training Reading** (60 minutes, 40 questions): five or six texts. Section 1 is short everyday texts; section 2 is short work-related factual texts; section 3 is one longer text of general interest. Sources can include advertisements, company handbooks and official documents.
- **Academic Writing Task 1**: describe a graph, table, chart or diagram, at least 150 words, about 20 minutes.
- **General Training Writing Task 1**: write a letter in response to a situation, at least 150 words, about 20 minutes.
- **Writing Task 2**, in both tests: discuss a point of view, argument or problem, at least 250 words, about 40 minutes.

The [General Training](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-general-training-test) page says you take that test at a test centre. The Academic page offers a centre or a remote Online sitting.

## What to book, and where to go next

For **Express Entry or another IRCC programme that names IELTS General Training**, book General Training at an authorised test centre. Do not book Academic, One Skill Retake, or IELTS Online for that application.

For a **degree programme** or for **professional registration**, start from Academic unless the institution or regulator tells you otherwise, and confirm the score it wants.
