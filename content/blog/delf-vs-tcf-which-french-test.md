You are choosing between two French exams from France Éducation international, and the honest answer to "which one?" is: it depends on who will read your result. A diploma that does not expire sounds better, but one important route in this post does not accept it at all.

This guide compares the **DELF** (and its higher-level sibling, the **DALF**) with the **TCF** for the three things people most often ask about: French citizenship and residence, university admission in France, and Canadian immigration. Every rule below comes from a government page that we read on 27 November 2026.

**Short answer.**
- For **French naturalisation, residence cards and DAP university applications**, both a DELF/DALF diploma and a recent TCF are named by the authorities. Pick the one that fits your level, timing and how long you need it to last.
- For **Canadian Express Entry**, DELF and DALF are not on IRCC's list. You need **TCF Canada** (or TEF Canada).
- For **Canadian citizenship**, IRCC accepts DELF B1 or higher and any DALF, as well as TCF Canada.

## DELF vs TCF: the core difference

![A two-column comparison of the DELF and DALF diploma and the TCF attestation: what you get, how long it counts, how results work and which skills are tested](/blog/delf-vs-tcf-which-french-test/diploma-vs-test.webp)
*Sources: étudiant.gouv.fr, Campus France Maroc and Service-Public, checked 27 November 2026. Chart by OpenExam Hub.*

- **The DELF and DALF are diplomas.** The French government's student site says a DELF is valid for life once obtained, and describes the DALF (levels C1 to C2) the same way. Campus France Maroc adds that each exam is marked out of 100, with 25 points per skill, and that you pass with 50/100 and at least 5/25 in every skill.
- **The TCF is a test.** Campus France Maroc describes it as a check of your French level at one moment in time. It produces an attestation that is valid for 2 years, placed on the six-level CEFR scale from A1 to C2.
- **The TCF comes in versions.** The authorities below name different ones: the general TCF with written expression for French universities, TCF Canada for IRCC, and TCF-Québec for Quebec. Always check which version your authority names before you register.

So a DELF is pass or fail at one level (for example B2), while a TCF tells you where you are. That matters if you are close to a threshold.

## Which test does each authority accept?

## French citizenship (naturalisation)

Service-Public (page marked as verified on 1 January 2026) says that for naturalisation by decree you must show French at **B2 or above, spoken and written**. The page shows the same B2 requirement for a declaration of nationality by marriage. The accepted proofs include:

- any diploma attesting at least B2 on the CEFR (alongside the national brevet and French diplomas at level 3 or above);
- a **TCF attestation** from France Éducation international issued **less than 2 years ago**;
- a TEF attestation from the Paris chamber of commerce, also less than 2 years old.

The Légifrance text of the arrêté of 22 December 2025, in force since 1 January 2026, adds conditions for tests: they must cover speaking, writing, listening and reading as separate exams, be taken in person at an exam centre on the same day in a single session, and the attestation is valid for two years. It also says that applications filed before 1 January 2026 stay under the earlier rules.

What this means in practice:

- A **DELF B2, DALF C1 or DALF C2** is a diploma at B2 or above, so it falls under the "diploma at B2" line. Service-Public does not name DELF in that list, so confirm with your prefecture if you want certainty.
- A **DELF B1 is below the bar** for naturalisation.
- A **TCF** is accepted only if it meets the test conditions above and is under two years old when your file is assessed. If your application takes time, a lifetime diploma avoids a re-sit.

## French residence cards

Since 1 January 2026, Service-Public says non-EU applicants for a first multi-year card must show **A2** and for a first resident card **B1**, for the categories concerned (applicants over 65 and several categories are not covered, and a civic exam is also required). The page, last verified on 10 February 2026, lists:

- as diplomas: **DELF, DALF and DELF Pro** from France Éducation international (and DUEF, DFP, DCL and French national diplomas at level 3 or above);
- as tests: the **TCF** from France Éducation international and the TEF, with an attestation valid for 2 years, covering speaking, writing, listening and reading in one in-person session.

At A2 or B1, a DELF at that level or higher is a permanent proof. A TCF is accepted but ages out.

## University admission in France

France's higher-education ministry publishes a notice for the **Demande d'admission préalable (DAP)**, the procedure for first-year licence applicants who live abroad and file through a French embassy. The 2027-2028 notice says:

- the language check is the **TCF Tout Public with a compulsory written expression test**, unless you are exempt;
- exemptions include holders of one of the higher-education ministry's French-language diplomas at **B2 or above** (Campus France Tunisie names the DELF B2 and the DALF C1 or C2 in its answer on who is exempt from the TCF for the DAP), and TEF candidates with 400/699 or more on the written tests plus the oral tests;
- the file deadline is **15 December 2026**, the TCF must be taken by **12 February 2027**, and an existing TCF attestation must still be valid on the filing deadline.

The French government's student site adds that some programmes may ask for a DELF B2, and some for a DALF at C1 to C2. For master's or doctoral applications, Campus France Maroc notes that each university chooses its own way of assessing French, so read the programme page.

If you already hold a DELF B2, you skip the TCF for the DAP. If you do not, the TCF with written expression is the route the notice describes.

## Canadian immigration: DELF is not the answer for Express Entry

### Express Entry

IRCC's Express Entry page lists the approved French tests as **TEF Canada** and **TCF Canada**. No DELF, DALF or general TCF is listed. Results must be **less than 2 years old** when you complete your profile and when you submit your application for permanent residence.

If Express Entry is your goal, read [TEF Canada vs TCF Canada](/blog/tef-canada-vs-tcf-canada) and convert your results with the [CLB and NCLC converter](/tools/clb-converter). It has TEF Canada and TCF Canada tabs for the French tests, plus IELTS General Training and CELPIP-General tabs for English. For why a DELF or DALF cannot go in an Express Entry profile, and how to move from a DELF B2 level toward an NCLC target, see [Can you use a DELF or DALF for Express Entry?](/blog/delf-dalf-express-entry).

### Canadian citizenship

IRCC's citizenship language-proof page (step 5) accepts the **DELF at B1 or higher**, **any DALF result**, and **TCF Canada** (and TCFQ) with **B1 or higher in listening and speaking**.

### Quebec skilled workers (PSTQ)

The Quebec page (updated 25 November 2026 on the page) lists TCF-Québec, TCF-Canada, TCF, DELF, DALF and the TEF family as accepted. The page asks for results that are **two years old or less** when you submit and, for DELF and DALF, a full scan of the diploma. As the page is worded, the two-year line applies to your results in general, so check with the ministry how it treats a diploma. Other Quebec programmes have their own pages.

## How to choose

1. **Start with the authority's own list.** The table above is a starting point. Open the page for your exact permit, programme or file.
2. **Express Entry? Skip the DELF.** Take TCF Canada or TEF Canada.
3. **Staying in France, with a long timeline?** A DELF or DALF at the level you need does not expire. A TCF can run out if your application is delayed.
4. **Short timeline, or you only need a score?** A TCF gives you a level even if you are below your target. A DELF is pass or fail at the level you sit, so choose that level carefully.
5. **University in France?** Check the DAP notice if you are a first-year licence applicant abroad. For other programmes, check the programme page.
6. **Think about re-use.** If you will need French for more than one purpose over several years, check each purpose's page first. A diploma helps in France; it does not help with Express Entry.
