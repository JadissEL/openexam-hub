People often search for “NCLC 7 TEF score” or “CLB 7 TCF Canada.” This page answers that narrower question: which scores correspond to **level 7** on each approved French test, and why that level shows up so often on [IRCC’s language](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) and [CRS](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/check-score/crs-criteria.html) pages. For the full conversion tables, use the [TEF Canada](/blog/tef-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry) and [TCF Canada](/blog/tcf-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry) guides. Official tables change; confirm the figures on IRCC before you submit a profile.

IRCC measures English in **CLB** and French in **NCLC**. The numbered levels are used side by side: CLB 7 for English, NCLC 7 for French.

## Why level 7 matters

Level 7 is not a universal “pass.” It is the figure IRCC uses in several different rules:

- **Federal Skilled Worker Program.** The first-official-language minimum is CLB 7 in English, or NCLC 7 in French, in **all four abilities**. The second official language only needs CLB or NCLC 5.
- **Canadian Experience Class.** For jobs in TEER 0 or 1, the minimum is CLB/NCLC 7 in all four abilities. For TEER 2 or 3, it is CLB/NCLC 5.
- **Federal Skilled Trades Program.** The French minima are lower: NCLC 5 in speaking and listening, and NCLC 4 in reading and writing. Level 7 is more than this programme requires, but it can still add CRS points.
- **CRS first official language.** IRCC’s table is labelled in CLB. CLB 7 is **16 points** per ability with a spouse or partner, or **17** without. That is a jump from **8** or **9** points at CLB 6. The table then keeps rising, up to 32 or 34 at CLB 10 or more. When French is your first official language, your NCLC level is the one you use with that grid; confirm on the [CRS page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/check-score/crs-criteria.html).
- **CRS French bonus.** NCLC 7 or higher in **all four** French skills adds **25 points** if your English is CLB 4 or lower (or you did not take an English test), or **50 points** if you also have CLB 5 or higher in all four English skills.
- **Skill transferability.** Some education and foreign-work combinations on the CRS page also use a CLB 7 threshold. Check that section if it applies to you.

For FSW and for the French bonus, **every skill** must be at 7 or above. A listening score in the NCLC 8 band does not rescue writing that is still at NCLC 6.

### TEF Canada — the numbers you type

On [IRCC’s TEF Canada table](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html), your language level is based on the **Équivalence ancien score** (Old score equivalence). IRCC says not to enter the **Score / 699** column; those figures “aren’t compatible with our system at this time,” and the wrong column may lead to a refused application. [Le français des affaires](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/en/candidate/test-evaluation-francais/tef-canada/results/) says the same.

For NCLC 7, the old-scale bands are:

| Skill | Équivalence ancien score (type this) |
|---|---|
| Speaking | 310–348 |
| Listening | 249–279 |
| Reading | 207–232 |
| Writing | 310–348 |

You can [read the full TEF Canada NCLC table, and which certificate column to use](/blog/tef-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry). The [TEF Canada exam guide](/exams/tef-canada) covers the official format.

### TEF Canada — Score / 699 (do not type this)

People searching “NCLC 7 TEF score” often mean the current **/699** scale. That is how the test is scored today, but it is **not** the column IRCC asks you to enter.

For tests taken **from 11 December 2023**, the official [Correspondance Score TEF – Niveau NCLC (PDF)](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/correspondance-tef-nclc.pdf) maps NCLC 7 to:

| Skill | Score / 699 (from 11 December 2023) |
|---|---|
| Speaking | 456–493 |
| Listening | 434–461 |
| Reading | 434–461 |
| Writing | 428–471 |

From 11 December 2023, reading and listening share a /699 range; writing and speaking have their own. The same PDF has a different “same range for every paper” column for tests taken between 1 October 2019 and 10 December 2023 (450–499 on each paper is NCLC 7). Use the column that matches your test date — and still do not type /699 figures into Express Entry.

### TCF Canada

[IRCC’s TCF Canada table](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) uses a different scale: speaking and writing out of 20, listening and reading out of 699. For NCLC 7:

| Skill | TCF Canada score |
|---|---|
| Speaking | 10–11 /20 |
| Listening | 458–502 |
| Reading | 453–498 |
| Writing | 10–11 /20 |

Do not reuse TEF numbers on a TCF file, or the other way around. The [full TCF Canada NCLC table](/blog/tcf-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry) also has levels 4 to 10, and notes that listening NCLC 9 starts at 523 while reading starts at 524. The [TCF Canada exam guide](/exams/tcf-canada) covers the official format. IRCC lists **TCF Canada**, not TCF tout public.
