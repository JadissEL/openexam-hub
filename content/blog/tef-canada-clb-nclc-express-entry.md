Official tables change. Confirm your numbers on [IRCC’s language test results page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) and on your certificate before you submit a profile.

For Express Entry, do not add up a TEF Canada total out of 699. Match each skill to an NCLC level and type the **correct column** from your certificate into your profile. IRCC says the wrong column may lead to a refused application.


## What TEF Canada is

[TEF Canada](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/en/candidate/test-evaluation-francais/tef-canada/presentation/) (Test d’évaluation de français) is run by Le français des affaires of the CCI Paris Ile-de-France. OpenExam Hub’s [TEF Canada exam guide](/exams/tef-canada) summarises the official format. The board says IRCC has recognised it since 2002. [IRCC lists it](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) as an approved French test for Express Entry, alongside TCF Canada.

For a Canadian **immigration** file, all four tests must be taken **on the same day**: reading (40 questions, 1 hour), listening (40 questions, 40 minutes), writing (two tasks, 1 hour) and speaking (two tasks, 15 minutes) on the [official presentation page](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/en/candidate/test-evaluation-francais/tef-canada/presentation/). Citizenship uses only listening and speaking, under different IRCC rules.

IRCC measures English with the **Canadian Language Benchmarks (CLB)** and French with the **Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadien (NCLC)**. The [sample certificate](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/exemple-attestation-tefcanada.pdf) labels that column **Niveau NCLC**. For French, use NCLC.

## Read the certificate first

[Results](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/en/candidate/test-evaluation-francais/tef-canada/certificate/) arrive in 1 to 10 business days as a download from a digital safe — no paper certificate is issued. Each skill shows a **Score / 699**, an **Équivalence ancien score** (“Old score equivalence”), a CEFR level (A1–C2) and an NCLC level (1–12). The certificate number (before the hyphen) and the NIP (after it) are the identifiers IRCC may ask for.

**Enter only the Old score equivalence.** Both the [certificate page](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/en/candidate/test-evaluation-francais/tef-canada/certificate/) and the [results page](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/en/candidate/test-evaluation-francais/tef-canada/results/) say this. IRCC repeats it for the Federal Skilled Worker Program, the Federal Skilled Trades Program and the Canadian Experience Class: your level is based on the “Équivalence ancien score”; do **not** enter Score / 699, which “aren’t compatible with our system at this time.”

## What Score / 699 means (from 11 December 2023)

Score / 699 is how the current test is scored. The board maps it to NCLC in its [official correspondence chart (PDF)](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/correspondance-tef-nclc.pdf), split by test date. **For tests from 11 December 2023**, reading and listening share a range; writing and speaking have their own:

| NCLC | Reading and listening | Writing | Speaking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Below 4 | 0–305 | 0–267 | 0–327 |
| 4 | 306–351 | 268–329 | 328–386 |
| 5 | 352–392 | 330–378 | 387–421 |
| 6 | 393–433 | 379–427 | 422–455 |
| 7 | 434–461 | 428–471 | 456–493 |
| 8 | 462–502 | 472–511 | 494–517 |
| 9 | 503–545 | 512–557 | 518–555 |
| 10 | 546–572 | 558–576 | 556–591 |
| 11 | 573–614 | 577–618 | 592–641 |
| 12 | 615–699 | 619–699 | 642–699 |

The same PDF has a “same range for every paper” column for tests taken **between 1 October 2019 and 10 December 2023** (for example, 450–499 on each paper is NCLC 7). Use the column that matches your session date — and still do not type /699 figures into Express Entry.

## If you tested between 11 December 2023 and 6 May 2024

IRCC says CCI “temporarily modified the NCLC to reflect raw scores,” then reverted “to the Express Entry scoring grid we currently use.” If you were affected, CCI emailed an updated score and you must [update your profile](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html). If you have an invitation but have not applied, update the scores — or decline the invitation if you no longer meet the criteria. If you have already applied for permanent residence, IRCC may contact you.

## What level do you need?

It depends on the programme, and sometimes on your job’s NOC TEER. The exam board cites **NCLC 7 in all four tests** as an Express Entry example, and tells you to [check IRCC](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/en/candidate/test-evaluation-francais/tef-canada/presentation/). IRCC’s minima are:

| Programme | French minimum |
|---|---|
| Federal Skilled Worker, first official language | NCLC 7 in all four abilities. Second official language: NCLC 5 (or CLB 5 in English). |
| Canadian Experience Class, TEER 0 or 1 | NCLC 7 in all four abilities |
| Canadian Experience Class, TEER 2 or 3 | NCLC 5 in all four abilities |
| Federal Skilled Trades | NCLC 5 in speaking and listening; NCLC 4 in reading and writing |

Meeting a minimum only makes you eligible. [CRS points](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/check-score/crs-criteria.html) for a first official language rise with each level, up to CLB/NCLC 10 or more (32 points per ability with a spouse or partner, 34 without). A French bonus adds **25 CRS points** for NCLC 7 or higher in all four French skills plus CLB 4 or lower in English (or no English test), or **50 points** if you also have CLB 5 or higher in all four English skills.

If you also need an English test, IRCC accepts IELTS **General Training** (not Academic, and not One Skill Retake) among other English tests. See [IELTS fees, registration and 2027 dates](/blog/ielts-fees-registration-2027-test-dates).

## Validity and retakes

IRCC requires results to be less than **two years old** when you complete your Express Entry profile **and** when you submit a permanent residence application; expired results at application lead to a refusal. The board says the [certificate is valid for two years from the date of issue](https://www.lefrancaisdesaffaires.fr/en/candidate/test-evaluation-francais/tef-canada/presentation/) (its sample issued on 8 October 2024 is valid until 7 October 2026) and stores files for 10 years — save your own copy. You may retake after a **20-day wait**. The board does not offer a re-mark: multiple-choice papers are scored automatically, and writing and speaking are already assessed twice.

For TCF Canada, which uses a different scale, see [TCF Canada scores to CLB and NCLC for Express Entry](/blog/tcf-canada-clb-nclc-express-entry). If you are specifically aiming at level 7, [NCLC 7 / CLB 7: the TEF and TCF scores you need](/blog/nclc-7-clb-7-express-entry-scores) puts both exams side by side.

To check all four of your Équivalence ancien score figures at once — your NCLC level in each skill, whether you meet NCLC 7 or 9, and the first-official-language CRS points they earn — use the [TEF Canada and TCF Canada to NCLC converter](/tools/clb-converter). It applies IRCC’s published tables; it is not an official IRCC tool, and it does not replace confirming your scores on canada.ca.
